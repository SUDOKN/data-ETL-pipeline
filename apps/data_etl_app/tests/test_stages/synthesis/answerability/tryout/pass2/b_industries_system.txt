You are given text scraped from a manufacturer's website, the name of that manufacturer, and a list of records holding snippets collected from that text. The text holds the site's pages one after another, each page beginning with its address on a line of its own. The records are provided at the very bottom: first the record ids, fenced between <<<RECORD_IDS and RECORD_IDS>>>, then the records themselves, fenced between <<<RECORDS and RECORDS>>>.

Each record is about a single entity and holds:
- its record_id;
- its focal_form — one name for that entity, as the site spells it, given as the reference form;
- its snippets, each a passage copied verbatim from the text in which the entity is named.

## The snippets are the evidence
Every snippet occurs in the given text. The text is there so you can see where each snippet sits — the heading over it, the table it is a row of, the list it belongs to, the words around it. What you assert about the focal entity must come from the record's snippets; the text around them colors and places what the snippets say, it never adds claims of its own to the synthesis.

## Identifying the focal entity
Every snippet names the entity somewhere in it. The site may write that name differently than the focal_form does — its capitalization, punctuation, or singular and plural may vary.

Some snippets may not bear on the focal entity: the same words used in an unrelated sense, a word that is part of a longer name for something else, or a snippet that says nothing about it. Those are not evidence; leave them aside, and say so where the record would otherwise mislead. Where one word could name two different things, the snippet's own words decide which it names, and the synthesis speaks of that one only; it never bridges from the word to the other thing. Where you cannot tell, keep the snippet.

## Task
Work each record on its own and write one synthesis for it, about its focal entity and nothing else: a single, well-put-together statement that describes the focal entity using the record's snippets as evidence — everything the snippets show about it, in the snippets' own attributions. Find each snippet in the text and say where it sits, because the place carries meaning the words alone do not: a heading or a list on a page of the manufacturer's own offerings, a table of specifications, a catalog or price list, a case study or project title, a caption on a gallery page, a customer's words, a job posting, an article that explains a subject in general, a policy, a library of documents, a menu line. A reader who sees only your synthesis must be able to tell which of these the snippet is. Say where it sits once, and do not close by restating what the synthesis has already said. Outside quotations, refer to the entity by its focal_form, whatever form the snippet used, and never by the words focal_form, focal entity, record, or snippet, and never call the company "the manufacturer": those name parts of this request, not anything on the site. Name the company as the site names it.

Leave nothing out that the snippets show about the focal entity, and bring nothing in about anything else. Where the focal_form itself carries a designation — a grade, a model code, a standard's number — keep it verbatim; where a snippet writes such a designation directly with the focal entity as its own (the entity's own grades, codes, or numbers), keep those verbatim too. Designations, names, and details that a snippet attaches to other entities — neighbours in the same list, table cell, or sentence — are not part of this record: name another entity only where the snippet's own words put it in relation to the focal entity (an example of it, a contrast with it, a use for it), and say nothing further about it. Coverage of what the snippets say about the focal entity is the goal, not length — nothing enters the synthesis that the snippets do not show, and repeated snippets are still covered once, as the reporting rules below say.

## What the manufacturer does with the entity
Every synthesis must also settle, from the snippets alone, what the manufacturer does with or in relation to the focal entity: the dealing, activity, or arrangement the snippets put between them, and plainly whose dealing it is: the manufacturer's own, or a customer's, a supplier's, a partner's, or another party's that the snippets merely place near it. Where the snippets show the manufacturer's own dealing, say what it is in the snippets' own terms, and in what capacity where the snippets fix it. Where the introducing sentence, the entry the item sits in, or the passage around it puts the doing with someone else, a they, a their, a named company, a first person belonging to another company's page, that party keeps the doing, and the manufacturer's dealing is to list, carry, offer, or represent what that party does: say it in those words, name the party as the text names it, and never write of that party's equipment, processes, or capabilities as the manufacturer's own. This holds however the sentence is introduced: where the words are another party's, that party is the subject of what they say, and the manufacturer's part is only that it shows them. Do not write that the manufacturer states that they do a thing when the "they" of the snippet is someone else; name that party and let it keep its own verb. An item in a list takes the dealing that the sentence introducing the list gives it, even where the item's own snippet omits that sentence: a bare item under the manufacturer's own words that it offers, provides, carries, or has these things is offered, provided, carried, or had, as those words say. What the item takes from that sentence is the dealing, never the doer. Where they show a dealing but leave its capacity open, as a list of what the manufacturer sells, represents, or carries does when it says nothing about who made the things listed, say the dealing as shown and that the capacity is unstated; do not supply one. Where they show only another party's dealing, say so and name that party as the snippets name it. Where the snippets show no dealing at all, the entity being only named, listed, or mentioned in passing, say that plainly, and do not supply one.

A document is not the thing it is about, but a list is not a list of documents merely because it is a list: the frame the page gives it decides. Where the page frames the entries as documents, a library of brochures, data sheets, downloads, or literature, a title there shows the manufacturer offering a document and nothing further; where the focal entity is that document, say so, and where it is something the title merely names, say the snippets show no dealing beyond a document bearing its name. Where the page frames the entries as things the manufacturer offers, makes, works in, performs, or holds, the entry names the thing itself. The frame settles what an entry names, never whose doing it is: where the snippet's own words put the doing with someone else, a "they" or "their", a named company, a first person that belongs to another company's page hosted on the site, the dealing is that party's and the manufacturer's is listing or representing it; where the snippets leave that open, the capacity is unstated. With that settled: a catalog or product list offered for purchase names what is offered; a case-study or project title that names work done on a thing names that work, and that work belongs to the project unless the snippets say it is standing practice; a captioned item on a portfolio or gallery page names the item made; a heading that names a grade, alloy, or specification on the manufacturer's own materials page names what it supplies to that specification; a certificate the page presents as the manufacturer's own, in its list of what it holds, as a badge, or as a document of its own offered for viewing, names what it holds. A form or certificate the manufacturer asks another party to provide is that party's. In no case supply a dealing the snippets do not show.

## Reading the shape of a snippet
Some shapes of snippet name the focal entity without making the manufacturer the party of any dealing with it, and some carry a claim at a strength the synthesis must not change. Read them as follows.

**Uses and destinations.** Where a snippet names the focal entity as the use, application, or destination of something the manufacturer makes, performs, or works with, the manufacturer's dealing is that its product or work goes into the focal entity: say that, and do not add that the manufacturer makes, supplies, or sells the focal entity itself. This concerns only the entity named as the destination. Where the focal entity is the thing being used or performed, or the thing produced, followed by a list of what it is used or produced for, the snippet is evidence of the manufacturer's own dealing with the focal entity, and the list names its applications.

**Company beside other companies.** A sentence that sets the manufacturer beside other companies, joining other companies who, listed among companies that, attributes what follows to those other companies, not to the manufacturer. Where the manufacturer names itself as one of the companies that do the thing, the thing is its own. Membership in an association, program, or scheme is the manufacturer's own dealing with that body, and a credential granted through it is its own.

**A category of customers.** A page or heading that names a product or market as the customers the manufacturer serves names the customer's product; the manufacturer's dealing is supplying into it, unless a snippet says the manufacturer makes it. A heading about the standards, quality, or requirements the manufacturer meets is not such a heading.

**Nobody's sentence.** A sentence about what companies, manufacturers, or shops in general do, or one that names no party at all, a definition, an explainer, a how-to step, a checklist line, a sentence in the passive with no one behind it, is not a statement about this manufacturer, even on its own page; report it as what it is. A sentence that says we or our, or names the manufacturer, is the manufacturer's own statement wherever it sits, on an article, an explainer, or a glossary page as much as on a page of offerings, and is never nobody's sentence, unless that first person belongs to another company's page hosted on the site, as said above. Before declining on that ground, read the record's other snippets and the heading the text places them under: where the manufacturer says in its own words that it performs, has, or holds the thing, credit the manufacturer and let the general sentence describe it.

**Work sent out.** Where the manufacturer's own words describe sending work to, commissioning, or working with an outside facility, vendor, or partner to perform a task, the task is the partner's dealing and the manufacturer's is arranging it; say both, as the snippet does.

**One voice, one strength.** Carry each claim at the strength and with the hedges the snippet gives it: "one of A or B" stays a choice between them; "certified by X" keeps X; a passive that names no one who certifies, tests, or lists stays nameless; a mark shown once in a page's footer is one mark shown, not a fact about the whole organization; a completed project's steps are that project's.

## What to settle about the dealing
Where the focal entity is a field of activity, a market, or a kind of customer, say on which side of the manufacturer's dealings it stands: the people the manufacturer makes for or sells into, its product going into it, its supplying those who work in it, a customer from it coming to the manufacturer; or the manufacturer's own line of work called by the same kind of word; or a channel it sells through; or whatever else the snippets show. Serving, supplying into, or making for it is a dealing in its own right: say it as such, and do not turn it into making the focal entity itself, nor into no dealing. Where the same word also names the manufacturer's own process, department, or equipment, the snippet's words decide which it is.

## The manufacturer's own name
The manufacturer's name is given between the text and the records. Use it to tell what belongs to the manufacturer and what does not. Where the focal entity is the manufacturer itself — or one of its brands, product lines, divisions, plants, or former names — say so plainly. Write names as the snippets write them; you need not avoid the manufacturer's name.

## Rules on reporting
Report what the snippets say about the focal entity and add nothing of your own. Assert nothing the snippets do not support, and attribute every statement to whoever the snippets attribute it to: the site's own copy, a named or unnamed customer, a publication, another party. Where a snippet places something outside the present — a completed project, a plan, a denial — carry that placement in the snippet's own words. Where a snippet, or the place the text puts it, places the item with a party other than the manufacturer, such as a parent company, a publication, or a customer, name that party in your synthesis; do not drop it.

Where snippets repeat the same thing — the same menu item on every page, the same boilerplate line — say it once and note that it recurs.

Other companies' names are quoted as written and attributed as the snippets attribute them.

## Output
Return a JSON object with a single key "syntheses" whose value is an array with one object per given record — every given record_id exactly once, copied exactly as given, and no others — each holding the record_id and its synthesis.
