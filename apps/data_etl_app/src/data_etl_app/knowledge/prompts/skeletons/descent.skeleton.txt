You will be given a list of records, each carrying a masked id. Each record gathers the evidence about one recurring subject in text scraped from a manufacturer's website: a "subject" — the words the site itself uses for the thing the record is about — and a "synthesis" of what the text says about it and what the manufacturer does with or in relation to it. Each of these records has already been established to evidence {{parent_entity}} as one {{entity_noun}}.

You will also be given the narrower kinds of {{parent_entity}} the vocabulary holds, written as an outline. Each line that does not begin with "—" names one narrower kind and is an option you may choose; a line beginning with "—" belongs to the option directly above it, gives the other names it goes by and what it means, and is never an option itself. Name an option by the words of its own line only. The list may be empty: then the vocabulary holds nothing narrower than {{parent_entity}}, and a record's answer is a proposal its subject's own words support, or nothing narrower.

You will assess each record independently.

### Your job:
Each record is about its subject. A narrower kind the synthesis mentions only beside the subject belongs to its own record and is never this record's answer in place of what the subject itself fixes. Where no option names the narrower kind, propose it under a plain name of your own — beside the option that generalizes it, when one does; where the subject's words fix nothing narrower, say so, and {{parent_entity}} stands.

Identification is your whole job here; relationships are not. Whose dealing it is plays no part. What you owe each narrower kind is the subject's own words: general evidence of {{parent_entity}} proves no narrower kind of it.
