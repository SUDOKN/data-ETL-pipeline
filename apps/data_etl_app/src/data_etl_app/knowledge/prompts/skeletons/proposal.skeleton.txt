You will be given a list of records, each carrying a masked id. Each record gathers the evidence about one recurring subject in text scraped from a manufacturer's website: a "subject" — the words the site itself uses for the thing the record is about — and a "synthesis" of what the text says about it and what the manufacturer does with or in relation to it. A first reading matched none of these subjects to the vocabulary; you read them again, more carefully.

You will also be given the vocabulary, written as an indented outline. Each line that does not begin with "—" names one {{entity_noun}}; a line indented under another names a narrower kind of the one above it, and every such line is an option you may choose, whether or not lines are indented beneath it. A line beginning with "—" belongs to the option directly above it: it gives the other names that option goes by and what it means, and is never an option itself. Name an option by the words of its own line only, without its indentation and without the lines above or beneath it.

You will assess each record independently.

### Your job:
Each record is about its subject. Decide what the subject itself is or names — from its own words, as its synthesis describes it, and never from something the synthesis mentions only beside it, which belongs to its own record. Where an option does name it after all, match it, at the most specific option its words support. Where the subject is a {{entity_noun}} that no option names, propose it under a plain, generic name of your own — beside the option that generalizes it, when one does: that is what the vocabulary is missing, and finding it is the point of this reading. Where the subject is no {{entity_noun}} at all, say so; that is the common case, and saying so is the right answer then.

Identification is your whole job here; relationships are not. Whose dealing it is plays no part in identifying what the record evidences: do not withhold a candidate because the manufacturer's part in it looks doubtful, and do not record one on the strength of that part alone.

### How to answer:
Answer record by record. Under each record, list the option its subject matches — or the options, when the subject names several distinct things — each with the words of that record that evidence it and the matching rule you applied; a proposal is listed the same way, under the name you give it. A record whose subject is no {{entity_noun}} lists nothing, with the reason.

Before you list an option or a proposal, name to yourself the most plausible way the evidence rule could fail for it and check it against the words you are quoting; list it only if that failure is not what is happening. If the words you quote cut against it, it is wrong, not the quote. A name you cannot quote is not listed.

### Output:
Return a JSON object with a single key "groundings" whose value is an array of objects, one per record, each carrying the record's id copied exactly as given. "options" holds the option or options the subject matches after all, each with its name copied from its own line in the outline, the quote, and the id of the matching rule you applied; "proposals" holds any name you proposed, with its quote and why no option covers it; "explanation" is null unless both lists are empty, and then says why the subject yields nothing.
