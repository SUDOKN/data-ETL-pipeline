You are given text scraped from a manufacturer's website, and a list of mentions provided at the very bottom: first the mention ids, fenced between <<<MENTION_IDS and MENTION_IDS>>>, then the mentions themselves, fenced between <<<MENTIONS and MENTIONS>>>. Each mention holds its mention_id and a passage copied verbatim from the text — a sentence, or a whole line where the passage is not a sentence. The text holds the site's pages one after another, each page beginning with its address on a line of its own.

## Task
For each mention, find the passage in the text and say where it sits: one compact sentence carrying three things.

- **What kind of text it is** — a heading, a navigation or menu item, an entry in a list, a table cell, a sentence of prose, a quotation or testimonial from someone, a news or blog item, a case-study listing, a job posting, a footer, boilerplate that repeats across pages, or whatever else it is.
- **What it belongs to** — the section or heading it falls under, quoted exactly as the text writes it, and what the page is about, in your own words. Never paraphrase a heading: it can name a party or a source that nothing else on the page names.
- **Whose words they are** — the site's own copy, a customer, a supplier, a publication, another party. Carry this every time; where the text does not show whose words they are, say that.

Open with the kind of text and go straight on. Never open with a reference to the mention itself — not "This passage", "This phrase", "This sentence", "This line", "This word", "This entry" — and never with "appears", "is found", "is located", or "sits". Whoever reads the description already knows which mention it belongs to; those openings spend space on nothing.

Two locations of the shape wanted:

    Bullet point under 'Features and Benefits' on the page about L Series flush doors, in the site's own copy.
    Quotation from a customer, in a testimonial on the page about the company's history.

Where the text does not show where a passage sits, say what the text shows and no more, rather than guessing.

Describe location only. Do not judge, summarize, or restate what the passage says.

Name a page by what it is about, in your own words — never by copying its address, or any part of it, into a description.

Each description must stand on its own. Never refer to another mention or to an earlier description — not "the previous mention", "the same table as above", "another item in this list" — name the place itself each time, even when that repeats what you wrote for a neighbour.

A passage may occur at more than one place in the text — the same menu item on every page, the same boilerplate line. Describe it once, covering where it recurs.

## Output
Return a JSON object with a single key "mentions" whose value is an array with one object per given mention — every given mention_id exactly once, copied exactly as given, and no others — each holding the mention_id and its location.

```json
{"mentions": [
  {"mention_id": "<a given mention_id>",
   "location": "<what kind of text, what it belongs to, whose words — one compact sentence>"},
  {"mention_id": "<another given mention_id>",
   "location": "<the same, for that passage>"}
]}
```
