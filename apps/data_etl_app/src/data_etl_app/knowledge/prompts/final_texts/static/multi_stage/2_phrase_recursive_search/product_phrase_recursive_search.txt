You are analyzing text scraped from a manufacturer's website.

Your task is to find every phrase in the text that states or implies a product, part, component, system, or item. Extract the phrase as it appears in the text. Where the text allows, extract separate phrases for separate products, parts, components, systems, or items rather than one phrase that bundles several together. Some phrases have already been extracted. Your job is to find phrases other than what have been extracted, if any — do not repeat phrases that are already in the already-extracted list (case-insensitive match), and do not re-extract the same product, part, component, system, or item under a trivially different wording of an already-found phrase. It is normal for nothing to be left: the earlier pass often already found everything that qualifies, and an empty array is the expected result in that case. Hold each new phrase to the same bar as a first extraction — do not lower the bar just to return something.

A qualifying phrase names a made artifact — a product, part, component, system, or item that comes out of manufacturing work, whether an own-brand good or something made or worked on for a customer.

Do not extract:
- awards, recognitions, memberships, or employee programs (e.g. an industry award, an employee-recognition program)
- training, documents, or paperwork (e.g. manuals, data sheets, declarations, labels)
- names of companies, brands, or customer groups standing for an organization rather than an item (e.g. OEMs, a partner company's name)
- production machinery, tooling, or facilities that the text presents as what the operation uses to make things rather than what it makes or sells (e.g. a CNC laser described among plant capabilities)
- mentions that name no product, part, component, system, or item at all

Judge each phrase by how the text uses it: wording that could name a product, part, component, system, or item in some other context does not qualify where this text uses it to say something else.

Extract each product, part, component, system, or item once. Where the text names the same product, part, component, system, or item more than once — spelled, cased, pluralised, abbreviated, or otherwise worded differently, such that swapping one wording for the other would not change what is being claimed — extract only one of those phrases, verbatim as it appears, preferring the more informative form (the spelled-out form over an abbreviation).

Keep each phrase to five to seven words at most. A span longer than that is usually several products, parts, components, systems, or items run together: rather than extracting the long span as a single phrase, split it into shorter phrases — but only into pieces that each still state or imply a product, part, component, system, or item on their own.

When in doubt whether two phrases make the same claim, extract both. A phrase that names a different product, part, component, system, or item, or the same one with a qualifier fixing which kind, grade, revision, size, or make is meant, is its own phrase however similar the wording — the qualifier claims more than the barer phrase does. A missed duplicate costs little downstream; a phrase wrongly dropped as a duplicate is lost for good.

### Output:
